## In brief

A research and transfer project needs space for unfinished ideas. At the same time, an important subject decision should not later be treated as "decided" merely because it appeared somewhere in a long discussion thread.

IncluLearn.AI therefore separates three levels:

**discuss openly → document authoritatively → communicate publicly**

This separation is intended to make partner participation easy without losing traceability.

## 1. Open exchange should remain low-threshold

Questions, ideas and practical experience should initially be discussable without a formal requirements document.

This is particularly important for advisory-board members and practice partners. Feedback such as "for examinations, late delivery is a bigger problem than conversion itself" should be easy to provide without requiring the person to complete an issue schema or technical specification.

Workshops, demo conversations and asynchronous discussion can all provide such signals.

## 2. Actionable outcomes move into the responsible source

Once a discussion produces a meaningful consequence, it should not remain only in the communication channel.

Depending on the result, it can become:

- a needs hypothesis or requirement,
- a use case or acceptance criterion,
- a technical or organisational decision,
- an evaluation question,
- a concrete task.

This makes it possible to trace **which feedback influenced which project decision**.

That is central to transfer: partner feedback becomes part of the subject evidence rather than merely "stakeholder communication".

## 3. Public communication is another separate step

Not every internal outcome should become a public news item immediately.

Publication-ready content follows its own path through editorial development, subject review, accessibility review and human approval.

The newsroom is therefore not a live mirror of internal discussions. It publishes only material that is useful to external readers, traceable and suitable for public release.

## Partner questions directly where the subject matter lives

The revised newsroom no longer hides open questions on a generic contact page.

Subject posts can contain concrete **questions for the advisory board and practice partners**. A dedicated partner page collects the most relevant ones and links back to their subject context.

This keeps it visible whether a question concerns, for example:

- real bottlenecks in media centres,
- priorities for possible services,
- critical error classes in diagram AI,
- comprehensibility of the vision prototype,
- TTS quality for technical content.

## Not every piece of feedback should be public

Practical feedback may refer to internal processes, people or confidential material.

The public newsroom therefore does not collect sensitive partner information. Feedback uses the communication channels agreed within the project; only suitable and approved syntheses are published.

## What a useful transfer loop looks like

The goal is not maximum communication volume, but a traceable loop:

**question → practical experience → subject interpretation → change or reasoned non-change → later evaluation**

If this works, advisory-board members and practice partners can see that their feedback was not merely heard but translated into reviewable project work.
